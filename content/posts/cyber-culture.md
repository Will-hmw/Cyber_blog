Some attacks have altered the history of IT security—and, at times, the history of the world itself. Here are **7 major cyberattacks**, ranging from an early experiment that spiraled out of control in 1988 to the shutdown of a major oil pipeline in 2021.


| | Year | Attack | Type |

| 🟦 | 2010 | **Stuxnet** | State-sponsored cyber-weapon |

## 🟩 1. Morris Worm (1988): The Internet’s First Worm


In November 1988, **Robert Tappan Morris**, a student at Cornell, launched an experimental worm onto ARPANET. Due to an error, the worm became **uncontrollable** and infected approximately **10% of the machines connected to the Internet** (nearly 6,000 computers).

| | |
|---|---|
| 🎯 **Target** | Machines connected to ARPANET |
| 👤 **Author** | Robert Tappan Morris (a student) |
| 💥 **Impact** | ~10% of the Internet infected |
| ⚖️ **Aftermath** | First conviction under the *Computer Fraud and Abuse Act* (CFAA) |

> 💡 **Key takeaway:** A simple bug in an experiment was enough to paralyze a portion of the network. Morris is now a professor at MIT.

---

## 🟦 2. Stuxnet (2010): The First Cyber-Weapon


Stuxnet was an attack developed by the **United States and Israel** (Operation *Olympic Games*) against the **Iranian nuclear program**. This highly sophisticated worm targeted centrifuges by altering their rotational speed via **Siemens programmable logic controllers (PLCs)**, all while concealing the issue from operators.

| | |
|---|---|
| 🎯 **Target** | Iranian nuclear centrifuges |
| 👤 **Author** | United States and Israel (widely accepted attribution) |
| 🧨 **Technique** | Utilized no fewer than **4 zero-day vulnerabilities** |
| 💥 **Impact** | Physical sabotage of industrial equipment |

> 💡 **Key takeaway:** Regarded as the **first official act of cyber-warfare**. Software code destroyed actual physical hardware.

---

## 🟪 3. Sony Pictures (2014): When a Film Triggers a Hack


A massive hack of **Sony Pictures** resulted in the leak of internal data, emails, and unreleased films. The attack is attributed to **North Korea**, in retaliation for the satirical film *The Interview*, which mocked Kim Jong-un. The **FBI** has confirmed this attribution.

| | |
|---|---|
| 🎯 **Target** | Sony Pictures Entertainment |
| 👤 **Perpetrator** | North Korea (confirmed by the FBI) ​​|
| 💥 **Impact** | Leaked internal data, emails, and films |

> 💡 **Key takeaway:** The first instance of a **destructive, state-sponsored cyberattack against a private** entertainment company.

---

## 🟥 4. WannaCry (2017): 230,000 machines in 4 days


This global **ransomware** infected more than **230,000 machines across 150 countries in just 4 days**. It utilized **EternalBlue**, an NSA exploit that was stolen and subsequently released by the *Shadow Brokers*. It crippled the **NHS** (UK National Health Service), **Renault**, and **FedEx**.

| | |
|---|---|
| 🎯 **Targets** | NHS, Renault, FedEx, and many others |
| 🧨 **Technique** | EternalBlue exploit (originating from the NSA) |
| 💥 **Impact** | 230,000+ machines, 150 countries, 4 days |

> 💡 **Key takeaway:** A 22-year-old researcher (**MalwareTech**) halted the outbreak by chance, by registering a domain name found within the malware's code. It could have been much worse.

---

## 🟧 5. NotPetya (2017): A fake ransomware, a real disaster


A Russian attack against Ukraine, disguised as ransomware. In reality, it was a destructive **wiper**: the data was **destroyed**, not encrypted with the possibility of recovery. The attack spread via the Ukrainian accounting software **M.E.Doc** and affected companies all over the world.

| | |
|---|---|
| 🎯 **Victims** | Maersk, Merck, Saint-Gobain, FedEx |
| 🤝 **Vector** | M.E.Doc software update |
| 💥 **Impact** | Maersk: ~$2 billion in losses. Over **$10 billion** in total damages |

> 💡 **Key takeaway:** considered the **most destructive cyberattack** in history.

---
