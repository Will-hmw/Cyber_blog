# 💻 7 Cyberattacks That Shaped History

> Some attacks have changed the history of computer security — and sometimes the history of the world itself. Here are **7 major cyberattacks**, from the first experiment that spiraled out of control in 1988 to a pipeline shutdown in 2021.

## 🟩 1. Morris Worm (1988)
### The first worm on the internet

In November 1988, **Robert Tappan Morris**, a Cornell student, released an experimental worm onto ARPANET. Due to a coding error, the worm became **uncontrollable** and infected roughly **10% of all machines connected to the internet** (nearly 6,000 computers).

| | |
|---|---|
| 🎯 **Target** | Machines connected to ARPANET |
| 👤 **Author** | Robert Tappan Morris (student) |
| 💥 **Impact** | ~10% of the internet infected |
| ⚖️ **Aftermath** | First conviction under the *Computer Fraud and Abuse Act* (CFAA) |

> 💡 **Key takeaway:** a simple bug in an experiment was enough to cripple part of the network. Morris is now a professor at MIT.

---

## 🟦 2. Stuxnet (2010)
### The first cyberweapon

Stuxnet was an attack developed by the **United States and Israel** (operation *Olympic Games*) against **Iran's nuclear program**. This highly sophisticated worm targeted centrifuges by altering their rotation speed through **Siemens PLCs** (programmable logic controllers), all while hiding the problem from operators.

| | |
|---|---|
| 🎯 **Target** | Iranian nuclear centrifuges |
| 👤 **Author** | United States and Israel (widely accepted attribution) |
| 🧨 **Technique** | No fewer than **4 zero-day vulnerabilities** |
| 💥 **Impact** | Physical sabotage of industrial equipment |

> 💡 **Key takeaway:** considered the **first official act of cyberwarfare**. Code destroyed real-world hardware.

---

## 🟪 3. Sony Pictures (2014)
### When a movie triggers a hack

A massive breach hit **Sony Pictures**: internal data, emails, and unreleased films were leaked. The attack was attributed to **North Korea**, in retaliation for the satirical film *The Interview*, which mocked Kim Jong-un. The **FBI** confirmed this attribution.

| | |
|---|---|
| 🎯 **Target** | Sony Pictures Entertainment |
| 👤 **Author** | North Korea (confirmed by the FBI) |
| 💥 **Impact** | Internal data, emails, and films leaked |

> 💡 **Key takeaway:** the first case of a **destructive cyberattack attributed to a nation-state against a private entertainment company**.

---

## 🟥 4. WannaCry (2017)
### 230,000 machines in 4 days

This global **ransomware** infected more than **230,000 machines across 150 countries in just 4 days**. It relied on **EternalBlue**, an NSA exploit stolen and later leaked by the *Shadow Brokers*. It crippled the **NHS** (UK healthcare system), **Renault**, and **FedEx**.

| | |
|---|---|
| 🎯 **Targets** | NHS, Renault, FedEx, and many others |
| 🧨 **Technique** | EternalBlue exploit (originating from the NSA) |
| 💥 **Impact** | 230,000+ machines, 150 countries, 4 days |

> 💡 **Key takeaway:** a 22-year-old researcher (**MalwareTech**) accidentally stopped the outbreak by registering a domain name found in the malware's code. It could have been far worse.

---

## 🟧 5. NotPetya (2017)
### A fake ransomware, a real disaster

A Russian attack against Ukraine, disguised as ransomware. In reality, it was a destructive **wiper**: data was **destroyed**, not encrypted with any hope of recovery. The attack spread through the Ukrainian accounting software **M.E.Doc** and hit companies worldwide.

| | |
|---|---|
| 🎯 **Victims** | Maersk, Merck, Saint-Gobain, FedEx |
| 🤝 **Vector** | M.E.Doc software update |
| 💥 **Impact** | Maersk: ~$2 billion in losses. Over **$10 billion** in total damages |

> 💡 **Key takeaway:** widely considered **the most destructive cyberattack** in history.

---

## 🟨 6. SolarWinds (2020)
### The largest supply chain attack

Russian hackers (**APT29 / Cozy Bear**) compromised SolarWinds' **build process** and injected a **backdoor** into its software updates. **18,000 customers** received the infected update.

| | |
|---|---|
| 🎯 **Targets** | US government (Treasury, DHS, Commerce), Microsoft, FireEye |
| 👤 **Author** | APT29 / Cozy Bear (Russia) |
| ⏱️ **Detection** | **8 months** after the initial compromise |

> 💡 **Key takeaway:** it was **FireEye**, itself a victim, that discovered the attack. Trusting an official update is no longer enough.

---

## 🟪 7. Colonial Pipeline (2021)
### The gasoline shortage

A **ransomware attack (DarkSide)** struck the largest fuel pipeline on the US East Coast. For **5 days**, gas stations across the East Coast ran dry. The company paid a **$4.4 million** ransom. Entry point: a **VPN without two-factor authentication** on an old account.

| | |
|---|---|
| 🎯 **Target** | Largest fuel pipeline on the US East Coast |
| 👤 **Author** | DarkSide group (ransomware) |
| 💰 **Ransom** | $4.4 million |
| 🚪 **Entry point** | VPN without 2FA, old account |

> 💡 **Key takeaway:** major political consequences, leading to stronger critical infrastructure security and the start of sanctions against ransomware ecosystems.

---

## 🧠 What These Attacks Teach Us

| Lesson | Illustrated by |
|---|---|
| 🔧 A simple bug can have massive consequences | Morris Worm |
| 🏭 Cyberattacks can cause physical damage | Stuxnet |
| 🩹 Patch fast — a stolen exploit turns against everyone | WannaCry |
| 🎭 A "ransomware" can conceal a destructive operation | NotPetya |
| 🔗 Trust in vendors is itself an attack surface | SolarWinds |
| 🔐 One account without 2FA can bring down a country | Colonial Pipeline |
